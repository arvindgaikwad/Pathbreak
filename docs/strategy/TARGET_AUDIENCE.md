# Pathbreak — Target Audience and Positioning

Last updated: 2026-08-06

## Primary audience

### Calm logic-puzzle players

- Age focus: approximately 25–55, while remaining accessible to younger and older players.
- Platform: Android phones first; tablets supported.
- Behaviour: plays in short sessions during breaks, travel, waiting, or before sleep.
- Motivation: wants light mental stimulation without timers, complex controls, or competitive pressure.
- Frustrations: excessive ads, accidental penalties, unclear dense boards, forced lives, and repeated levels.
- Desired experience: easy to understand, satisfying to clear, readable, reliable, and respectful.

### Casual brain-training players

- Enjoys Sudoku, sorting, block, maze, nonogram, match, and tap-away games.
- Responds to “logic,” “focus,” “spatial thinking,” and “daily puzzle” language.
- Wants gradual challenge rather than a steep difficulty spike.
- Values hints and tutorials but dislikes being treated as incapable.

### Older and accessibility-sensitive players

- Needs larger touch targets, high contrast, readable type, and zoom on dense levels.
- Is more likely to abandon after a false touch penalty.
- Benefits from Relax Mode, reduced motion, and clear sound/haptic controls.
- This segment can become a genuine advantage if accessibility is designed into the level rules rather than added late.

## Secondary audience

### Puzzle mastery players

- Wants optimal solutions, move counts, stars, hard levels, and daily challenges.
- More willing to replay levels and compare performance.
- Should be served through Challenge Mode, not by making the default experience stressful.

### Satisfying-game viewers and short-video audiences

- Attracted by clean path-removal animations, chain clear moments, and “find the first move” challenges.
- Important for organic acquisition through short vertical videos.
- May install for visual satisfaction before becoming a puzzle player.

## Not the initial target

- Competitive esports or synchronous multiplayer players.
- Children-focused audiences requiring child-directed monetization and policy design.
- Players seeking story-heavy adventure content.
- Hardcore strategy players expecting deep systemic simulation.
- Users requiring a social guild, chat, or account ecosystem.

## Player jobs to be done

1. “Give me a satisfying five-minute mental reset.”
2. “Let me solve something clever without feeling rushed.”
3. “Do not punish me because the touch target was unclear.”
4. “Give me enough variety that the game does not feel generated and repetitive.”
5. “Let me play offline and resume exactly where I stopped.”
6. “Let me choose relaxation or mastery.”

## Positioning statement

For mobile players who enjoy calm logic puzzles but are frustrated by inaccurate controls and aggressive ads, Pathbreak is a directional-path puzzle that combines precise touch handling, readable boards, original chapter mechanics, and respectful monetization. Unlike generic arrow-puzzle clones, Pathbreak offers distinct Relax and Challenge experiences and treats player trust as a core feature.

## Product pillars

### Fair

- Ambiguous taps do not remove a life.
- Hit areas are larger than visible lines.
- The nearest intended path is selected reliably.
- Ads never appear immediately after failure.

### Readable

- Board density is validated.
- High-contrast mode and scalable UI are supported.
- Path overlap and direction remain visually clear.
- Dense levels can support zoom later.

### Calm

- No forced timer in the main mode.
- Motion and sound remain restrained.
- Relax Mode removes lives and failure pressure.

### Fresh

- Chapters introduce original mechanics.
- Levels are solver-verified and human-reviewed.
- Weekly packs and daily puzzles extend the game after launch.

### Respectful

- Rewarded ads are voluntary.
- Interstitial frequency is capped.
- A permanent remove-ads purchase is available.
- Progress works offline and does not require an account.

## Initial personas

### Persona A — The evening relaxer

- Plays 10–20 minutes after work.
- Wants calm visuals, no timer, and low ad pressure.
- Likely to buy remove-ads if trust is established.

### Persona B — The daily puzzle regular

- Opens once or twice per day.
- Wants daily challenges, trophies, and visible progress.
- Retention depends on reliable fresh content.

### Persona C — The precision-sensitive player

- May be older, use a small device, or have motor/vision constraints.
- Leaves quickly if the wrong path is selected.
- Values high contrast, large targets, and safe tapping.

### Persona D — The optimizer

- Replays for three stars and fewer moves.
- Wants harder boards, statistics, and challenge streaks.
- Can become a long-term player if mastery systems are meaningful.

## Research plan before soft launch

### Qualitative test: 10–15 players

Recruit across:

- 3–4 casual puzzle players aged 18–30.
- 3–4 players aged 30–50.
- 2–3 players aged 50+.
- 2–3 people who rarely play puzzle games.

Observe without teaching:

- Can they explain the rule after Level 1?
- Do they understand blocked paths?
- Can they find Hint and Restart?
- Do they accidentally select the wrong path?
- Does the menu feel like a game?
- At which level do they become bored or confused?

### Quantitative closed test

Track:

- Tutorial completion.
- Completion rate by level.
- Blocked taps and ambiguous taps.
- Hint use.
- Restarts.
- Session duration.
- Return on Day 1 and Day 7.
- Device resolution and performance.

## Messaging to test

- “Find the clear path.”
- “A calm logic puzzle with precise controls.”
- “No timers. No accidental penalties.”
- “Play offline, one smart move at a time.”
- “Relax or chase the perfect clear.”

Avoid unprovable claims such as “improves intelligence,” “prevents cognitive decline,” or “best brain-training game.”
