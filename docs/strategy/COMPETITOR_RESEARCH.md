# Pathbreak — Competitor Research

Last researched: 2026-08-06

## Scope

This document separates competitors into three groups:

1. Direct 2D directional-path competitors.
2. Closely adjacent tap-away and arrow puzzle games.
3. Broad casual logic-puzzle competitors competing for the same player time.

Public user counts are unavailable. Google Play install bands and App Store rating counts are used only as scale signals, not as active-user counts.

## How many competitors exist?

There is no stable exact number because listings launch, rename, disappear, and localize continuously. A practical working estimate from current store search is:

- 10–20 directly comparable 2D arrow-escape games with visible store presence.
- Dozens of lower-scale clones and regional variants.
- Hundreds of adjacent tap-away, block-removal, screw, pin, sort, and directional puzzle games.

Pathbreak should therefore assume a crowded category with low mechanic-level defensibility.

## Public audience-size interpretation

Do not write “Easybrain has 10 million users.” The correct statement is:

- Google Play displays a 10M+ lifetime install band for Easybrain's Arrow Puzzle.
- This does not disclose active users, retained users, paying users, or unique people.
- App Store ratings and Google Play reviews indicate engagement scale but cannot be converted reliably into MAU without paid intelligence data.

## Competitor pattern audit

Most direct competitors converge on the same package:

- Arrow/path clears only when its forward route is open.
- Wrong tap costs a life.
- Hint highlights a valid move.
- No timer / relaxing positioning.
- Hundreds or thousands of levels.
- Ads and in-app purchases.
- Minimal white or pastel interface.
- Daily challenges and trophies in stronger products.

This means matching the standard feature list will not create a market advantage.

## Recurring weaknesses visible in public reviews and listings

- Touches register on the wrong path when boards become dense.
- Lines become difficult to read on small screens.
- Ads interrupt the calm experience.
- Rewarded ads occasionally fail to grant rewards.
- Games restart, freeze, or lose state.
- Content claims feel repetitive despite large level counts.
- UI and store pages are often nearly indistinguishable.

## Recommended competitive position

Pathbreak should own five promises:

1. Precise touch selection with no penalty for ambiguous taps.
2. Relax Mode and Challenge Mode rather than forcing lives on everyone.
3. Original chapter mechanics introduced gradually.
4. Respectful ad frequency and a fair permanent remove-ads purchase.
5. Strong offline reliability, save recovery, and small-screen readability.

## Competitive moat

The moat is not the mechanic. It is the system around it:

- Level editor and automated validation.
- Solvability solver.
- Difficulty estimation using simulated and player data.
- Original mechanics and chapter design.
- Fast weekly content production.
- Analytics-informed balancing.
- Consistent art direction.
- Player trust from precise input and restrained monetization.
